## 🔍 What Is It?

**SWOT is a way of writing down what you are good at, where you struggle, what good chances exist out there, and what dangers are coming — all in four boxes — so you can make a smarter plan.**

SWOT is a thinking tool that helps you look at any situation — a new business, a sports team, or even yourself — from four angles at once. The letters stand for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. You write each one in its own box so nothing gets forgotten.
The first two boxes — Strengths and Weaknesses — are about YOU. They are things inside your control that you can work on and change. The last two — Opportunities and Threats — are about the world around you. You cannot always control those, but knowing they exist means you can prepare for them.
Most people only think about what they are good at, or only worry about what could go wrong. SWOT makes you look at all four angles at the same time, so your plan covers the whole picture. That is why coaches, business leaders, and even school clubs use it before making big decisions.

## 🧸 Think Of It Like This

**The Soccer Tryout Map**

Imagine you want to make the school soccer team, so you grab a piece of paper and split it into four boxes. In the first box you write your STRENGTHS: you are the fastest runner in class and have played for three years. In the second box go your WEAKNESSES: your left foot is weak and you tire out in the second half. In the third box sit your OPPORTUNITIES: the coach just announced the team badly needs a speedy winger — exactly your best position. The fourth box holds the THREATS: two other kids trying out are also lightning-fast and have been doing extra training all summer. Now you can see the whole field at once and make a real plan — practise your left foot this week and arrive early on tryout day to show the coach your speed.

## 🌍 Real World Example

Before launching the iPhone, Apple's leaders could have used SWOT like this: Strengths — loyal fans and beautiful design skills; Weaknesses — zero experience making phones. The Opportunity was that millions of people wanted a smarter phone, and the Threat was that giant phone companies like Nokia already ruled the market. Seeing all four boxes together helped them decide to go for it and focus hard on design to stand out from everyone else.

## 🎯 Try It Yourself

Pick one thing you want to get better at — a sport, a school subject, a hobby — and fill in your own four boxes: what are your two biggest strengths, your two biggest weaknesses, one opportunity coming up soon, and one threat you should watch out for?
